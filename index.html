      What the estimate assumes

      The likely range assumes each gauge is good to about 10 percent while the tunnel runs and 15 percent when it's off: the typical disagreement between neighboring gauges with nothing entering or leaving between them. There's roughly a 2-in-3 chance the true flow falls inside it. It is widest when the canals take most of the river, because gauge errors add up while the flows cancel.

      • Dry Cedar Creek enters between Chipeta Lake and the park and isn't gauged. The page adds its typical flow from 34 USGS measurements (1991–2001): about 16 cfs while the tunnel runs, easing to about 2 cfs over the week after it shuts off. Piped laterals may have lowered it since.
      • The Stark-Volkman Ditch has reported live only since September 2026. When it's silent, the live estimate uses its typical diversion for the month from DWR's official records (2020–2025); the history charts use its actual daily records.
      • Storm drains and irrigation return flows in town still aren't counted. Water takes about 2 hours to get from the Road Bridge gauge to the park, so the readout runs about 2 hours ahead of the park; the Outlook chart is drawn in park time.
      • The City's park page shows an estimate built the same way, minus Dry Cedar Creek and the Stark-Volkman Ditch, so the two can differ by about 40 cfs in summer.
      • No independent flow measurements at the park exist to check against. Volunteer readings in public databases track the Colona gauge, not the park.
      • The canal stations go quiet in winter. The console then shows the last complete reading and says so.
      • The history charts go back five years. They use DWR's official daily diversion records for the three canals where those are published (through October 2025 for the M&D and Loutsenhizer canals), which also correct gauge readings that stuck while a canal shut down. In winter, with the tunnel off and the canal stations quiet, the canals count as off, as DWR's records show every December through February. Days when the canal gauges read more than the river carries are left blank.

      How the outlook works

      Summer storms raise the town run mostly through creeks Ridgway Reservoir doesn't control. Cow Creek is the largest; Horsefly and Dry Cedar creeks join below every gauge.

      • Cow Creek routing. Colona follows Cow Creek and the dam release about 2 hours later, and Road Bridge follows Colona about an hour after that, so the page can route the latest readings 3 to 5 hours ahead at the park. Fit hour by hour on 72 summer storms (2016–2026) and tested a year at a time, it cut the average miss 3 hours ahead, while Cow Creek was rising, from 46 cfs (assuming no change) to 36. Timing is its strength; size is loose. The real rise typically came in at 0.8 times the routed one, and 2 times in 3 between 0.1 and 2 times, so the line is drawn at 0.8 with that range.
      • CBRFC forecast. NOAA's river forecast for the Road Bridge gauge (UNBC2) includes forecast rain. The page nudges it to match the latest reading, easing that correction out over about a day, then runs it through the same sum as the estimate.
      • Radar odds. A model that turned radar rain into Cow Creek flow failed when tested a year at a time: at the start of the 24 biggest rises of 2021–2026 it called a real rise for none of them, because radar shows about the same rain for storms that do nothing and storms that send Cow Creek up hundreds of cfs. Radar does tell the odds. Fit hour by hour on the same years, the chance of a 20+ cfs Cow Creek rise within 12 hours rises with the last 6 hours of rain, the 72 hours before them and the last hour, and the 72-hour rain matters: at any 6-hour total, the wetter half of past hours had 1.5 to 2.5 times the odds. Tested a year at a time, its odds held up (hours given 20–35% saw a rise 32% of the time). The shaded window marks when such a rise would reach the park, with its typical size.
      • Reservoir operators cut the release in 21 of 72 past storms, which shrinks a rise. The routing picks up a cut once it shows at the dam gauge; the CBRFC forecast may not.
